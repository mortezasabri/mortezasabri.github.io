Where does the 1.216 Mb scored region come from?

A benchmark number only means something relative to the region it is computed over. The two assays target very different amounts of genome, so the only fair comparison is the DNA they both cover, where GIAB can judge, minus what the panel is designed never to call.

Step 1 — the panel is not "a small exome," it is a different design

Only 68% of the panel falls inside the exome. The rest is the panel's intronic / promoter / CNV-backbone / fusion / MSI content the exome does not target — the non-overlap is itself the headline. The circles are schematic; the exome is ~20× the panel, so a true-area Venn would shrink the panel to a dot:

shared 1.347 Mb   panel-only 0.624 Mb (outside the exome — cannot be compared)   exome-only (the panel does not target it)

That block-listed slice is exactly why the naive mutual sensitivity looks low for the panel (87.04%): those are not misses, they are sites the panel is designed never to call. Remove them and the two assays are equivalent (the attempted row).

Where they differ — and why it isn't a capability gap

Every one of the 602 GIAB truth variants in the mutual region, classified by which assay detected it (panel vs the like-for-like WES-somatic callset). The story is entirely in the block list.

The exome's extra recoveries come bundled with false positives, in the same repeat territory. The panel makes 0 FP (0 in the block list); the exome makes 13 FP (13 in the block list). So the exome trades 60 true repeat recoveries for 13 false calls there; the panel trades both away for clean precision. Textbook sensitivity-vs-specificity in low-complexity DNA — a deliberate design choice, not a quality gap.

Depth (the real-world advantage HG002 cannot exercise): the panel reads each shared site about 2.3× deeper than the exome (median 1255× vs 539×), the basis of its ~5% VAF limit of detection — decisive on real low-purity/FFPE tumors, irrelevant on a germline sample where every variant sits at ~50/100% VAF.
