01 The question

“Which one is better?”
— better at what?

A targeted cancer panel and a whole-exome test do different jobs. Asking which is “better” only makes sense per capability. So we measured the one thing they truly share — small-variant calling — on identical ground, and mapped the rest to the clinical question.

02 The verdict

Neither wins outright — they’re built for different jobs

For the small variants they share, the two are equivalent (~99.7% sensitivity) — assay choice is not a small-variant-quality question.

For solid-tumor profiling & biomarker-driven therapy (MSI · TMB · HRD · CNV · fusions, low-VAF FFPE) → the panel. For breadth, rare/novel targets & germline questions → whole-exome.

03 What each assay is

A focused searchlight vs. a wide floodlight

TSO500 — targeted panel

  • 523 cancer genes, ~2 Mb of the genome
  • Very deep (~1250×) + UMI error correction
  • Built-in MSI, TMB, HRD, CNV, fusions (DNA+RNA)
  • Locked, clinically-validated pipeline

WES — whole-exome

  • ~20,000 genes (all coding exons), ~41 Mb
  • Moderate depth (~150–540×), no UMIs
  • Broad discovery; true germline genotypes
  • Flexible, lab-developed for these biomarkers

Only 68% of the panel even overlaps the exome — the rest is non-coding content (fusions, CNV backbone, MSI loci) an exome never captures.

08 The panel’s real edge

Depth & limit of detection — for real tumors

TSO500
~1255× · UMIs · LoD ~5% VAF
WES
~540× · LoD ~10–15%

HG002 can’t show this (a germline variant sits at 50–100% VAF, so both saturate). It matters on real tumors: subclonal, low-purity, or FFPE variants at 5–10% VAF — exactly where the deep, UMI-corrected panel wins.

09 The tumor-biomarker layer

Where the panel is designed to win

Panel advantage

  • MSI — validated tumor-only, 98% vs PCR
  • HRD — SNP-backbone + validated GIS*
  • CNV & fusions — DNA+RNA, validated
  • Low-VAF FFPE detection

Be careful / WES-favored

  • TMB — WES is the reference (not a panel win)
  • Breadth — rare/novel drivers
  • Germline / hereditary findings

Honest caveat: HG002 is germline — it has no MSI/TMB/HRD, so these rest on the validation literature, not this benchmark. *HRD needs the separate TSO500 HRD product.

12 Read the fine print

What this benchmark can — and can’t — say

HG002 is a germline control. It validates small-variant detection only — no MSI, TMB or HRD were measured; those rest on assay design + literature.
Like-for-like. Both are DRAGEN tumor-only, allele-level (squash-ploidy), GIAB v4.2.1, PASS, AF ≥ 5%, on the mutual region.
TMB is a WES-reference metric — don’t present it as a panel win.
Operational data is local. Cost, turnaround, sample input aren’t scored — add your lab’s numbers.
HRD needs the separate TSO500 HRD product, not the base panel benchmarked here.
13 Bottom line

Choose by the clinical question, not a sensitivity decimal

For shared small variants the two are equivalent. Everything that separates them is what else the assay delivers.

TSO500 is the default for validated solid-tumor profiling and actionable biomarkers; whole-exome complements it for breadth and germline questions.
