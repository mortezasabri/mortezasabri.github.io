01The verdict · which is better, in what sense

Neither wins outright — they're built for different jobs

Bottom line for the MTB

For small-variant calling in the genes they share, the two workflows are equivalent (~99.7% sensitivity) — so assay choice is not a small-variant-quality question. But they are not interchangeable: the panel and the exome deliver different things.

For routine solid-tumor profiling and biomarker-driven therapy — MSI, TMB, HRD, CNV, fusions, and low-VAF FFPE calling — the TSO500 panel is the better fit. For breadth, novel or rare targets, and germline / hereditary questions, whole-exome is. The scorecard and decision guide below place each case.

Mapped to the clinical questions a tumor board actually faces. This is general best-practice guidance — the final assay choice is an MTB / QM decision that also weighs local validation, cost, and turnaround.

04Method · why the naïve number lies

A benchmark is only as honest as its region

The two projects measured different things: a curated 2 Mb of cancer genes versus 41 Mb of whole exome. Comparing their headline percentages directly is apples-to-oranges. The fair test scores both on the same positions — built by narrowing to the intersection.

07Where the panel is genuinely better

The advantage isn't small variants — it's the tumor layer

Read this first HG002 is a germline control. It has no somatic mutations, no microsatellite instability, no HRD scar — so none of TMB / MSI / HRD can be measured on it, by either assay. This section is a design-and-validation-literature comparison, not a result from these runs.

MSI — microsatellite instability

PANEL WIN

A validated tumor-only model over 130 pre-selected homopolymer loci with a locked threshold, 98% concordant with MSI-PCR. WES actually has more sites (~3,500) — so the edge is clinical calibration, not site count.

TSO500 130 loci · locked cutoff · 98% vs PCR  ·  WES needs a self-built panel-of-normals

HRD — recombination deficiency

PANEL WIN *

Genomic-instability scoring (LOH + TAI + LST) needs genome-wide allele balance. The TSO500 HRD product adds a dedicated SNP backbone + the licensed Myriad algorithm. WES HRD (scarHRD) is research-grade and CNV-noisier.

* Requires the separate TSO500 HRD configuration — not the base assay benchmarked here

TMB — mutational burden

NOT A PANEL WIN

Be honest here: whole-exome is the reference standard panels are validated against (TSO500 vs WES R² = 0.93). By footprint, WES is the more stable estimator. The panel's edge is operational — locked cutoff, FFPE robustness, cost — not accuracy.

Don't list TMB as a panel "win" in the validation report

Low-VAF limit of detection

PANEL WIN

The panel spends its budget as very deep, UMI-corrected coverage on a small region — ~5% VAF validated LoD. It matters only on real tumors (subclonal, low-purity, FFPE); on HG002 every variant is at 50–100% VAF, so both saturate.

Median depth here TSO500 1255× vs WES 539× · UMIs vs none
08Scope · caveats · data

How this was measured — and what it cannot tell you

Read the scorecard with these boundaries in mind, so the decision rests on what the data actually supports.

  • HG002 is a germline reference. It validates small-variant detection only. It has no somatic mutations, no MSI, no HRD scar — so MSI / TMB / HRD were not measured here; those scorecard rows rest on assay design and the cited validation literature.
  • Like-for-like comparison. Both callsets are DRAGEN tumor-only somatic, scored allele-level (vcfeval --squash-ploidy) against GIAB v4.2.1, PASS, AF ≥ 5%, on the mutual region — identical treatment for both.
  • The deciding factors are the non-measured ones. Small-variant equivalence is the measured result; the biomarker, breadth and pipeline-validation differences — which drive the recommendation — come from the literature and assay design, not this benchmark.
  • Operational data is local. Cost, turnaround and sample-input requirements are lab-specific and are not scored here; add your own numbers before a final decision.
  • HRD needs the HRD product. The base TSO500 assay benchmarked here does not include the HRD SNP-backbone pool.
